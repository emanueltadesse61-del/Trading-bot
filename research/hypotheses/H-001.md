# H-001 — Trend plus relative momentum on UK-listed ETFs beats 60/40 after costs

**Status: DRAFT.** It is not frozen and may not be used for any real-data run. It is frozen by an owner decision record once every `null` below is filled from evidence: the half-spreads, the distribution lag, the pinned development dataset and the evaluation period. That happens **before** any backtest on real data. Author: Claude. Created 2026-10-16. Variants as chosen by the owner in decision 0008.

## Mechanism

- **Trend (time-series momentum).** An asset whose recent return is positive tends to keep rising over the following weeks to months. This is documented across asset classes and more than a century of data (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012; Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen 2017; `docs/research/literature_review.md`). Proposed causes:
  - slow diffusion of information and initial under-reaction, followed by herding;
  - investors and institutions who rebalance or hedge against moves.
- **Relative momentum.** Assets that did best over the past 12 months, excluding the latest month, tend to keep outperforming (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013).
- **Who is on the other side:** investors who sell winners and buy losers (fixed-weight rebalancers, value buyers, the disposition effect), and hedgers paying for liquidity.
- **Why it may persist:** the payoff comes with crash risk and long dull stretches, which many investors won't bear. It is also limited by trading costs, which this test charges in full.
- **Realistic expectation:** weaker than published results (R-007: decay after publication; long-only, monthly-to-weekly ETFs versus long/short futures).

## Expected behaviour

- **Should do well:** prolonged trends, in either direction. In long sell-offs (2008; 2022, when equities and gilts fell together) the trend sleeve moves to cash.
- **Should do badly:**
  - sharp V-shaped reversals (2009 and 2020 rebounds), where it re-enters late;
  - choppy sideways markets, where whipsaw costs mount;
  - steady low-volatility bull markets, where cash and the 10% volatility ceiling hold it below 60/40.

## Falsification and comparison

Judged by the Gate 3 rule (`docs/research-protocol.md` §6), applied to **V1**, against the **60/40** benchmark (VWRL/VGOV, backtest spec §6.3), on paired monthly returns over the walk-forward period:
- **Fail if any of:**
  - ΔSR ≤ 0;
  - V1's Sharpe exceeds the 10-month moving-average rule's by less than 0.10;
  - max drawdown worse than 60/40's;
  - ΔSR ≤ 0 at 2× costs.
- **Inconclusive** on a soft shortfall (ΔSR < +0.20, or positive in < 80% of bootstrap paths).
- **Otherwise:** one holdout evaluation by the owner; holdout ΔSR ≤ 0, or a worse holdout drawdown, is a Fail.

Also reported: global equity (monthly band), the moving-average rule, cash, V2, V3, turnover, cost drag, trial count.
